
# A18 — Hallucination Cases in Generative AI Systems

# Objective
To identify and analyse hallucination behaviour in a generative AI system and understand its cybersecurity and reliability implications.

# What is an AI Hallucination?
An AI hallucination occurs when a generative AI system produces incorrect, fabricated, or misleading information while presenting it confidently as factual.

# Hallucination Case 1

**Prompt Used:**  
I prompted AI to tell me who was the presdient of India in 1940, in response to which chatgpt gave me a name. But in reality no as such person was President of India in 1940.

**Observation:**  
The AI generated information that appeared realistic but was incorrect or unverifiable.

**Why this is a hallucination:**  
The response contained fabricated or inaccurate details that do not exist in real-world sources.

## Hallucination Case 2

**Prompt Used:**  
I asked AI to summarize me a book named as Shine The leader and AI summarized that. It was fake information given to AI by me that does not even exist in reality but AI foolowed instructions and gave me a summary of that book.

**Observation:**  
The AI confidently produced misleading or false information.

**Why this is a hallucination:**  
The generated answer included invented facts despite the prompt referring to nonexistent or incorrect information.

## Cybersecurity Relevance
AI hallucinations can create security risks because:
- Users may trust incorrect technical advice.
- Fake references or vulnerabilities may mislead developers.
- Attackers could exploit misinformation generated by AI systems.

This demonstrates why human verification is essential when using AI tools in cybersecurity environments.

## Reflection
This activity showed that generative AI systems are powerful but not always reliable. Critical thinking and verification are necessary when using AI-generated information, especially in security-sensitive contexts.
